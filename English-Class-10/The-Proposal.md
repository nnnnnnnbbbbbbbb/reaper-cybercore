# The Proposal — Class 10 CBSE

**Author: Anton Chekhov**

> Original revision notes. This is not a reproduction of the NCERT chapter.

## Overview
*The Proposal* is a one-act comedy about Ivan Lomov, who visits his neighbour Stepan Chubukov to ask for the hand of Chubukov’s daughter, Natalya. Instead of calmly discussing marriage, the characters quarrel over land and dogs.

## Complete summary
Ivan Vassilevitch Lomov, a nervous landowner, visits his neighbour Stepan Stepanovitch Chubukov dressed formally. He has come to propose marriage to Chubukov’s daughter, Natalya. Chubukov is delighted when he understands Lomov’s purpose.

When Lomov and Natalya are left together, Lomov begins a polite conversation and mentions Oxen Meadows. He claims the land belongs to his family, but Natalya insists that it belongs to hers. Their disagreement becomes a loud quarrel. Lomov becomes highly excited and unwell, and he leaves. Chubukov then tells Natalya that Lomov had come to propose marriage. Natalya is upset that the proposal may be lost and asks her father to bring Lomov back.

Lomov returns. The conversation soon turns to their dogs, Guess and Squeezer. Lomov praises Guess, while Natalya argues that Squeezer is better. Chubukov joins the dispute, and the argument becomes increasingly ridiculous. Lomov becomes unwell and collapses. When he recovers, Chubukov quickly brings the couple together and encourages them to accept the proposal. They agree to marry, but almost immediately begin arguing about the dogs again.

## Main characters
- **Ivan Lomov:** A wealthy landowner who is nervous, excitable and eager to marry Natalya.
- **Natalya Stepanovna:** Chubukov’s daughter; strong-minded and argumentative, and also interested in marriage.
- **Stepan Chubukov:** Natalya’s father; welcomes the proposal but joins the quarrels.

## Events in order
1. Lomov visits Chubukov to propose marriage.
2. Chubukov welcomes the idea enthusiastically.
3. Lomov and Natalya quarrel about Oxen Meadows.
4. Lomov leaves; Natalya learns the purpose of his visit and asks for him to return.
5. They quarrel about Guess and Squeezer.
6. Lomov becomes unwell and collapses.
7. Chubukov brings them together and the proposal is accepted.
8. The couple starts arguing again, even after agreeing to marry.

## Central idea and themes
The play makes fun of pride, ego, material concerns and unnecessary arguments. The characters value being right more than maintaining a pleasant relationship. Themes include marriage, social status, property, vanity, human foolishness and comedy.

## Key terms
- **Proposal:** an offer, here an offer of marriage.
- **Landowner:** a person who owns land.
- **Quarrel:** an angry disagreement.
- **Impulsive:** acting suddenly without careful thought.
- **Engagement:** a formal agreement to marry.
- **Excitable:** easily made emotional or agitated.

## Exam practice
**Why did Lomov visit Chubukov?** He came to ask for Natalya’s hand in marriage.

**What was the first quarrel about?** Ownership of Oxen Meadows.

**Why did Natalya ask Lomov to return?** She learned that he had come to propose marriage and did not want to lose the opportunity.

**What was the second quarrel about?** The merits of their dogs, Guess and Squeezer.

**Why is the ending humorous?** The couple accepts the proposal but immediately resumes arguing.

## Title and message
The title is suitable because Lomov’s marriage proposal drives the action of the play. The play suggests that ego and petty disputes can damage relationships, and that patience and mutual respect matter more than winning every argument.
