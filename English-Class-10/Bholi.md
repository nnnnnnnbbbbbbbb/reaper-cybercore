# Bholi — Class 10 CBSE

**Author: K.A. Abbas**

> Original revision notes. This is not a reproduction of the NCERT chapter.

## Overview
Bholi, whose real name is Sulekha, is a girl who is neglected and mocked because she stammers and has marks left by smallpox. A kind teacher gives her confidence through education. As an adult, Bholi stands up against a greedy bridegroom and chooses self-respect.

## Complete summary
Bholi is the youngest daughter of Ramlal, a prosperous farmer and village revenue official. When she was very young, she fell from her cot and suffered an injury. At two years old she had smallpox, which left marks on her face. She also stammered. People considered her slow and made fun of her, and her family paid less attention to her than to her siblings.

A girls’ primary school opens in the village. The Tehsildar asks Ramlal to send his daughters to school. Ramlal’s wife suggests sending Bholi, thinking that education will not affect her marriage prospects. On her first day, Bholi is frightened, but her teacher treats her kindly, encourages her to speak and gives her a book. The teacher promises that education will help her become confident. Bholi begins to attend school and gradually gains confidence.

Years later, her parents arrange her marriage to Bishamber Nath, a wealthy older grocer. During the wedding, Bishamber sees Bholi’s face and demands five thousand rupees as dowry. Ramlal is humiliated and begs him to proceed without the demand. Bholi hears the conversation and refuses to marry a greedy man who insults her family. She tells her father not to worry and says she will look after him and become a teacher. Her education has given her the courage to defend her dignity.

## Main characters
- **Bholi (Sulekha):** The central character; initially timid, later educated, confident and self-respecting.
- **Bholi’s teacher:** Kind and patient; encourages Bholi and helps her gain confidence.
- **Ramlal:** Bholi’s father; arranges her marriage and is distressed by the dowry demand.
- **Ramlal’s wife:** Bholi’s mother; initially has low expectations for Bholi.
- **Bishamber Nath:** A wealthy grocer who demands dowry and is rejected by Bholi.

## Events in order
1. Bholi is injured as a baby and later suffers from smallpox.
2. Her stammering and facial marks lead others to underestimate her.
3. A girls’ school opens and Bholi is sent there.
4. Her teacher encourages her and gives her confidence.
5. Years later, her parents arrange her marriage to Bishamber.
6. Bishamber demands five thousand rupees as dowry.
7. Bholi refuses to marry him.
8. She chooses self-respect and decides to become a teacher.

## Central idea and themes
Education can transform a person by developing confidence, knowledge and independence. The story also criticises dowry, discrimination and judging people by appearance. Themes include women’s empowerment, self-respect, kindness, equality and the influence of a good teacher.

## Key terms
- **Stammer:** to speak with involuntary pauses or repeated sounds.
- **Tehsildar:** a local revenue/administrative officer.
- **Dowry:** money or property demanded or given in connection with marriage.
- **Self-respect:** a sense of one’s own dignity and worth.
- **Empowerment:** gaining confidence and ability to make decisions.

## Exam practice
**Why was Bholi called a backward child?** She had suffered an injury as a baby, had marks from smallpox and stammered, so people wrongly considered her unintelligent.

**How did the teacher help her?** The teacher was patient, encouraged her speech and showed her that education could make her confident.

**Why did Bishamber demand money?** After seeing Bholi’s pockmarks, he used her appearance as an excuse to demand dowry.

**Why did Bholi refuse the marriage?** She would not accept a marriage based on greed and disrespect.

**What did Bholi decide at the end?** She would care for her father and become a teacher.

## Message
A person’s worth is not determined by appearance or speech. Education can build confidence and independence. Everyone deserves dignity, and no one should accept humiliation or dowry demands to secure a marriage.
