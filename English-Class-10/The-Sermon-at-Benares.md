# The Sermon at Benares — Class 10 CBSE

**Based on the teachings of Gautama Buddha**

> Original revision notes. This is not a reproduction of the NCERT chapter.

## Overview
The lesson presents the truth that death is unavoidable and grief is part of human life. Through Kisa Gotami’s experience, Gautama Buddha teaches acceptance, wisdom and freedom from excessive attachment.

## Complete summary
Siddhartha Gautama was born into a royal family and lived in comfort. His father tried to protect him from the unpleasant realities of life. During visits outside the palace, Siddhartha saw an old man, a sick person, a funeral procession and a monk. These sights made him understand that old age, illness and death affect everyone. He left his royal life in search of truth. After deep meditation and enlightenment, he became known as the Buddha and began teaching others.

Kisa Gotami was grieving because her only son had died. Unable to accept his death, she carried the child from house to house asking for medicine. Someone directed her to the Buddha. He told her to bring mustard seeds from a house where no one had ever died. She visited many homes. People were willing to give her mustard seeds, but every family had experienced the death of a loved one. She gradually understood that death visits every household and that her suffering was not unique. The Buddha’s lesson helped her accept the universal truth of death.

## Characters
- **Gautama Buddha:** Former prince Siddhartha; after enlightenment, he teaches about suffering, impermanence and wisdom.
- **Kisa Gotami:** A mother grieving for her dead child; her search helps her understand that death is universal.

## Events in order
1. Siddhartha sees old age, sickness, death and a monk.
2. He renounces royal life and seeks spiritual truth.
3. He attains enlightenment and becomes the Buddha.
4. Kisa Gotami loses her only son and seeks help.
5. The Buddha asks her to find mustard seeds from a house untouched by death.
6. Her search shows that every family has experienced loss.
7. She understands the truth and begins to accept death.

## Central idea and themes
Death is a natural and unavoidable part of life. Acceptance, wisdom and control over attachment can help people face suffering. Themes include impermanence, grief, compassion, detachment and enlightenment.

## Key terms
- **Sermon:** a religious or moral teaching.
- **Renunciation:** giving up worldly possessions or pleasures.
- **Enlightenment:** deep spiritual understanding.
- **Grief:** deep sorrow after a loss.
- **Mortal:** subject to death.
- **Alms:** food or money given to people in need.
- **Lamentation:** an expression of deep sorrow.

## Exam practice
**Why did Siddhartha leave the palace?** He had seen old age, sickness and death and wanted to understand human suffering.

**Why did the Buddha ask for mustard seeds?** The condition that no one in the household had ever died would help Kisa Gotami discover that death is universal.

**What did Kisa Gotami learn?** Every family experiences loss; she was not alone in her grief, and death cannot be avoided.

**What is the message?** Accept life’s realities and develop wisdom rather than being overwhelmed by attachment and sorrow.
